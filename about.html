[ About ]

Hi, I'm Ryan. I help organizations that serve the public figure out what to build, and then I help them build it.

Most of my work is with government agencies, nonprofits, and the firms that partner with them. I've been in software for about fifteen years, and the last eight have been on federal and nonprofit programs. These days I run Ryan Koch Advisory, a veteran-owned small business. I'm based in Busan, South Korea, I can cover any US time zone, and I'm happy to get on a plane when the work calls for it.

[ Why I Do This ]

When I was in the Army Reserve, the software I had to use was awful. All of it. That stuck with me, and a lot of my career since comes down to a pretty simple goal: make sure the systems people depend on don't suck.

The bigger version of that is access. Applying for a benefit, finding child care, moving your family to a new duty station. Each of those runs through a system, and each system decides how hard it is to get through. I care a lot about lowering those barriers.

[ What I Do ]

  • Discovery and research. I start by talking to the people a service is for and the staff who run it, then learn what systems and data it depends on. Sometimes the answer turns out to be policy or process instead of technology. That's still an answer.
  • Prototypes and first versions. I like getting a thin, working version in front of real users early. I still write code (Python, Django, Go, React, Postgres, AWS).
  • Modernization and technical leadership. Fractional engineering or IT leadership. Replacing legacy systems while people are still using them. Figuring out whether delivery is actually getting better, plus incident and change management.
  • Security and AI governance. NIST RMF and 800-171, authority-to-operate work, and AI governance that teams will actually follow. I hold CISM and CASP+.
  • Building capacity. Hiring processes, leveling, procurement coaching, and governance practices that keep working after I'm gone.

I'm happy to lead a team or be a hands-on member of one.

[ How I Work ]

I think the best decisions get made close to the work. The people doing it usually have better ideas than whoever's in charge, so most of leadership, for me, is facilitation. Set up the conversation, keep it focused, and point the team's creativity at the hard problems. If I'm mostly choosing between good proposals my team brought me, things are going well.

I also bring a policy lens to technical work. Ideas like incentive design and administrative burden show up in almost every system decision. Success metrics turn into targets fast, and every form field is a choice about who carries the burden. I like asking those questions early, before they get expensive.
