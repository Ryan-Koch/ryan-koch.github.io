[ Overview ]

I research how public services work and prototype ways to improve them. Then I help the teams that run them keep improving after I'm gone.

Most engagements start with talking to the people a service is for and the caseworkers and staff who serve them, then learning what systems and data the service depends on. Only after that do I decide what to change. Sometimes the fix is technology. Sometimes it's policy or process. I'll tell you which one I think it is.

I work with government agencies, nonprofits, and the digital services firms that partner with them, either directly or as a subcontractor on a larger team. I'm happy to lead the work or be a hands-on member of the team doing it.

[ Engagements ]

Discovery and research

Interviews and field observation with users and frontline staff, plus technical discovery of the systems and data underneath. You get a clear picture of where the problems are and what to do first.

Prototypes and first versions

Thin, working versions tested with real users early. I write the code myself or alongside your team.

Modernization and fractional leadership

Replacing legacy systems while people are still using them, and part-time engineering or IT leadership while you need it. Roadmaps, delivery measurement, vendor and prime coordination, incident and change management.

Security and AI governance

NIST RMF and 800-171, authority-to-operate support, least-privilege access design, and AI governance that teams will actually follow.

Building capacity

Hiring processes and rubrics, leveling, procurement coaching, and governance practices that keep working after the engagement ends.

[ Past Work ]

Ryan Koch Advisory is a new practice. Here's what I've done in each of these areas at Truss (on MilMove, for U.S. Transportation Command) and at Child Care Aware of America (CCAoA).

Discovery and research

  • Interviewed and observed people using MilMove on quarterly base visits, working alongside our UX researchers.
  • Joined regular interviews with military families, caseworkers, and quality control staff at CCAoA. Those interviews showed families were confused by long checklist emails, which moved a document management system up the roadmap and led to a plain-language rewrite of onboarding.
  • Measured administrative burden at CCAoA by counting document touch points before, during, and after modernization. The new system cut them by 20%.
  • Wrote MilMove's guide to measuring team impact, using program evaluation methods like difference-in-differences to test whether process changes actually worked.
  • Wrote a research briefing supporting Ohio's K-12 computer science bill (HB 170), which became law.

Prototypes and first versions

  • Before architecting a new case management system for MilMove, built a thin end-to-end version of the approval flow and had caseworkers process test moves on it.
  • Helped turn around MilMove delivery ahead of go-live. Got the customer to set the first milestone as enabling the first 200 moves, and coached teams to bring lean options with clear trade-offs. Teams were delivering consistently within three months.
  • Led the phased modernization of CCAoA's military child care fee assistance platform through its first production release, while families kept using the old one.
  • Mapped how 49 states and DC publish child care licensing data, then built an open pipeline that normalizes it for 250,000+ providers and publishes weekly. (code, dataset, Childcare Mosaic)

Modernization, integration, and procurement

  • Practice lead for four teams and 28 engineers on MilMove, a $20M/year contract.
  • Owned MilMove's integration with another federal contractor. Wrote the partner API versioning policy, negotiated its support policy on the customer's behalf, and reorganized teams so it had a dedicated owner.
  • Worked both sides of procurement: led engineering teams writing government proposals at Truss, and reviewed vendor proposals as the technical member of CCAoA's leadership team.
  • Ran a 15-person product and engineering department at CCAoA on a ~$5.5M budget.

Governance and capacity

  • Interviewed CCAoA's department heads, ran AI policy sessions with the full senior leadership team, and wrote the organization's first AI governance framework.
  • Built CCAoA's security and technology governance as a repeatable set of practices: security policies, project intake, decision records, and incident and change management.
  • Was the escalation point for MilMove's authority to operate, and cut an outside contractor's production access down to least privilege.
  • Built CCAoA's UX design and research practice, and advised local child care resource and referral agencies on technology tooling.
  • Co-presented a featured session with Nava at CCAoA's 2026 Symposium on AI pilots for early childhood systems, for an audience that included state child care agency staff.
  • Designed an open hiring process for MilMove engineering leads with a rubric and structured questions. Applicants went from 3 for one opening to 10 for two.
